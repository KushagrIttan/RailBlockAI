The Problem

Railway track maintenance is planned manually, and the gaps are brutal to find.

🚧
Finding windows
Planners manually scan timetables for gaps long enough to safely close a line — an error-prone, hours-long process.
⚠️
Shadow blocks
Closing a section forces adjacent trains to be re-routed or held — knock-on effects called shadow blocks.
🧠
No prioritization
Which maintenance job matters most under a deadline? Today's process doesn't rank by risk or impact.
planner@control · 08:00 batch
# today's manual gap search on DLI-GZB
finder gaps --section DLI-GZB-DN --min 45min
✔ 1 gap found 09:28–13:27 (239 min)
replanner insert CASE-TRACK-001
✘ conflicts with 3 services — shadow blocks
# …repeat for every case, every day.
Our Solution

One pipeline: timetable → gaps → ML-ranked schedule → human sign-off.

01
Frozen timetable
Public schedule snapshot per corridor (DLI-GZB, NDLS-NDB) with train classes and headways.
→
02
Gap detection
Merge occupied stretches per line, add safety headway, derive usable possession windows.
→
03
ML ranking
Calibrated model scores each maintenance case by urgency, risk, and resource profile.
→
04
Best-fit placement
Greedy scheduling with rescue-swap backtracking, resource capacity, shadow-block detection.
→
05
Human approval
Judge approves or rejects each block; verdicts persist to a decision log.
